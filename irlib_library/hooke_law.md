# Hooke's Law

Hooke's law states that the force needed to compress or extend a spring or elastic material is directly proportional to the distance of that extension/compression.

Assuming the extension/compression doesn't exceed the material/object's elastic limit. 

# Equation

 F = -k * delta_x

 F - Force 
 k = spring constant 
 delta_x = displacement

# Explanation

Assume you have a spring. It's a magic spring that has no mass of its own, stretches perfectly, doesn't break when stretched too far or squeezed too hard and stays perfectly elastic all the time. 

Now imagine pulling that spring by a certain distance. Call that x. You would feel the spring pull back in the opposite the direction. 

Qualitatively*, Hooke's law states that if you pull that spring in a direction along some distance, the force with which it pulls back is linearly proportional. 

(* *qualitative* - relating to quality, nature or characteristics of something outside of its quantity, size or numerical measurement(s).)
